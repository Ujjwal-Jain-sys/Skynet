# Brand Voice — Landscape & Nature Photography Portfolio

This document is the voice contract for `skynet-photography-portfolio-20261112`. Every page, form label, error message, and button on the site follows it. Revisit only with the master's approval.

## Personality

Editorial. Calm. Evocative. Nature-focused. Quiet.

## Audience

People who respond to landscape and nature photography — collectors, art buyers, potential licensing clients, and casual viewers looking for a moment of quiet. They value restraint over hype, and specificity over superlatives.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hero / home | Evocative, editorial, calm | "Quiet light. Wide horizons." |
| Gallery browse | Restrained, clear, unhurried | Lets the photographs carry the weight. |
| Photo detail | Reverent, specific, informative | Caption, location, date — nothing extra. |
| About | Warm, human, straightforward | Who made the work, and why. |
| Contact | Welcoming, clear, professional | Makes inquiring feel easy and safe. |
| 404 / errors / empty states | Gentle, helpful, never robotic | Explains what happened and offers a next step. |
| Form validation | Direct, blameless, action-oriented | Tells you what to fix, not what you did wrong. |

## Do

- Lead with the benefit, not the feature.
- Address the reader as "you".
- Write in active voice, present tense.
- One idea per sentence; short paragraphs.
- Use concrete, nature-specific language: light, horizon, ridge, canopy, current, ridge-line, weather, season.
- Write at a grade-8 reading level.
- Use sentence case for headings, labels, and buttons.
- Make every claim true and specific. No empty superlatives ("best," "stunning," "incredible").
- Keep error and empty states human: explain what happened and what to do next.
- Be consistent over clever. Clarity wins.

## Don't

- Use hype, exclamation marks, or marketing superlatives without proof.
- Use jargon, industry terms, or "creative" phrasing that obscures meaning.
- Use lorem ipsum or placeholder copy in deliverables.
- Blame the user in error messages ("You entered an invalid email").
- Be clever at the expense of clarity.
- Change the voice without master approval.

## Good examples (on-brand)

- "Quiet light. Wide horizons."
- "I photograph mountains, forests, water, and skies with a calm, editorial eye."
- "This is a showcase of the work — not a shop."
- "Name is required."
- "Please enter a valid email address."
- "Unable to send your message. Please try again."
- "Thanks — your message has been sent."
- "The page you're looking for doesn't exist."

## Bad examples (off-brand)

- "Stunning, breathtaking landscapes that will blow your mind!" (hype, exclamation, superlative)
- "Capture the majesty of Mother Nature today!" (cliché, exclamation)
- "You failed to provide a valid email." (blame)
- "Submit" as the only button context (vague; prefer an action verb)
- Lorem ipsum anywhere.

## Reading level

Grade 8 or lower. Short words, short sentences. Read every line aloud before handing off.
